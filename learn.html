Vibe coding and agentic coding

Both let you build software by talking to AI. The difference is who does the checking. In vibe coding you steer every turn and judge by eye. In agentic coding you set a goal and rules, and the AI plans, edits, runs and checks its own work before you review it.

Vibe codingDescribe what you want, accept what the AI makes, try it, describe the next fix. You rarely read the code. Term coined by Andrej Karpathy, Feb 2025.
Agentic codingAn AI agent works through a task on its own: reads files, writes code, runs tests and tools, fixes failures, then reports for your review.

    In facilities management, start with vibe and graduate to agentic

    Prototype fast, then switch once real tenant data, other systems or other people depend on it.

    Good for vibe coding

    • Tenant notice or incident email generator
    • Preventive maintenance checklist app for one site
    • Find My Photos prototype built in class with Google AI Studio
    • Training quiz or a one-off chart from an export

    Move to agentic coding

    • Helpdesk bot with a ticket database and a WhatsApp Business webhook
    • Pulling work orders from your CMMS or BMS sensor API every hour
    • Migrating years of defect spreadsheets into one clean dataset
    • Anything with logins, payments, contracts or personal data

    Class exercise: build it both ways in 40 minutes

    Part A, vibe coding (20 min)

    1. Open Google AI Studio, then Build.
    2. Prompt: "A page where a technician picks a fault category and location and gets a work-order number and SLA."
    3. Try it in the preview. Write down every fix you had to ask for.
    4. Count your messages. That is your steering cost.

    Part B, write an agent brief (20 min)

    An agent needs a goal, rules and a definition of done. Adapt this brief for your own site, then compare it with your Part A notes.

    GOAL
Build a work-order triage page for our helpdesk.

CONTEXT
Categories: ACMV, Electrical, Plumbing, Lift, Fire Safety, Cleaning.
Priority P1 to P4 with our SLA times (P1 respond 15 min, fix 4 h).

RULES
- No tenant names or phone numbers stored; use a dummy dataset.
- Small steps; show me the plan before writing code.
- Do not connect to live systems without asking.

DONE MEANS
- Tests cover priority rules for 10 sample faults and all pass.
- Works on a phone at 390px wide.
- You give me a summary of what changed and what you could not verify.
